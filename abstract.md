A real noncommutative generating series admits a finite-dimensional analytic
differential representation if and only if its Lie rank is finite and it
satisfies the convergence condition on ordered products of Lie polynomials.
The Lie rank is the least representation dimension. Two representations
of that dimension are equivalent by an analytic change of coordinates
fixing the origin.

The algebraic foundation consists of shuffle multiplication, the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem, and formal coordinates on the annihilator
of a finite-codimension Lie subalgebra. The analytic argument uses
factorial estimates for iterated vector fields, convergence of residual
series, and the analytic inverse function theorem. Convergence is expressed
by geometric bounds on commutative power-series coefficients. Both the
output and all vector-field coefficients are required to converge.

The submission includes the real-coefficient algebraic foundation and its
proofs, so it has no dependencies on other archive submissions. The source
paper records the three approved corrections to the analytic statements
and estimates.
